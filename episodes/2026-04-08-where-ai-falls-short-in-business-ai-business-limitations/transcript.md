# Where AI Falls Short in Business #AI #Business #Limitations

I'd like you to think about what happens in a business as having three layers. The first layer is the tokenizable cognition that I just talked about. Drafting, analysis, coding, etc. This is the layer where AI has made marginal cost collapse. A firm can now produce effectively unlimited quantities of this work at near zero incremental cost. The second layer is judgments and accountability. Someone has to decide which of the drafts look good. Someone has to sign off on the analysis. Someone has to own the outcome if the recommendation turns out to be wrong. This is not getting cheaper. This layer requires human judgment and it requires humans who are authorized to be accountable. AI can generate the options. It cannot own the accountability for the decisions. The final layer gets back to the atoms part of the economy. It's physical execution. Maybe that's installation. Maybe that's repair. Maybe that's face-to-face caregiving. No matter how good AI gets at generating text, it cannot show up at your house and fix your furnace. The layer is constrained by the physical world in ways that do not yield to the software.
