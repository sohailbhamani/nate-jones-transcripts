# Fable 5.2 Is really good and really expensive. So, when should you use it?

You may try Fable 5.2 and decide it doesn' change much about your work or that the improvement is too expensive to use every day. But what happens if someone on your team finds a way to use it that saves a ton of work for you or a competitor can now deliver something better with the same number of people? I got early access and it's really really good. I've been trying to program an e-ink display for over a month now and I had made three earlier attempts and they hadn't give me what I wanted and I tried across multiple different labs, multiple different AI models and harnesses. I couldn't get there. Fable went through hundreds of files that I'd previously worked on as part of the project, came back with a design I liked immediately and then went ahead and built it and it got it right. At the same time, business owners I talk to are looking at what Fable 5.2 costs and saying this is real expensive for doing real work. Where do you apply this firepower? I think that question helps explain why we're getting a K-shaped AI economy. And by K-shaped, I mean two groups pulling apart over time, right? One is making rapid gains while the other sees much less change. The labs can use better AI to help build the next generation. They're definitely on the upper slope of that K. But in an ordinary business, you have to find work where the improvement is worth what it costs and finding that work, when I talk to a lot of business owners, that's a job in and of itself. That's a big difference if the labs start moving faster and faster. And the people behind the projection AI 2027 think that that is true, that superhuman AI researchers are likely to arrive next year. Their reading of the research evidence puts one measure of acceleration about five months ahead of their own projections. In other words, if you look at it as an exponential curve, it's getting closer. I am picky about design. I care about which information appears first, what gets emphasized, how pieces fit together. This is the story of my e-ink struggles. I have tried multiple times with different models including Fable 5.1 and I still wasn't happy with the ability of an AI to put together an informational display on my desk that was not terrible. But this one, this one worked. I was able to give Fable 5.2 all of my earlier attempts. I was able to try to articulate the bar, but mostly to be honest with you, I just said it sucks, it doesn't work, fix it. And I admitted to Fable 5.2 that part of the job was reading between the lines of failed prior attempts, looking at my prior feedback in previous Claude work where I said no a lot and inferring what it was that I was pointing at when I described the things that were wrong with prior design. That's pretty complicated work. That is Fable being asked to infer my intent. Fable being asked to infer what I want when I have the good sense to see it when I see it, but I have difficulty articulating it proactively. And actually for a lot of knowledge work, if you're editing, you know it when you see it and you know it when it's wrong and you yell about it until it gets right. So that's not an uncommon thing. And so I told Fable, this is the stuff that isn't right. And it came back with a design that exceeded my expectations. That's useful because getting from I know this isn't right to here's what we ought to build is a ton of the work. The model helped with that thinking very concretely. It made sense of what I'd already tried and gave me something I felt confident saying yes to partly because the model got it right, but also partly because I'd spent so much time thinking about it. This is not cognitive surrender. This is not me saying, oh finally it's the fourth attempt, I'm done. This is me saying I've spent hours thinking about getting this right. Okay, finally we're here. And so being baked in the context as a human also helped me to be able to be clear about when I got it right. And I think that's something we don't want to miss as AI gets better. Then the conversation started getting long in the tooth. Fable noticed that its context window was getting heavy and almost full. This was all on the design thread. And it made a handoff file proactively. 5.2 did and it told me to start a fresh window for the build. That is a very handy behavior. Basically, it found a natural segue between design and build and said, I really think we should have a separate thread and I would perform better in a separate thread. Here's a handoff file. Please go ahead and use this thread and build it. I have been preaching that as a best practice for a long time. It's really nice to see AI just say, hey human, you should just do this. And it just worked. I was able to pick up the handoff file. I was able to say, please build this. And it went through and was actually able to put it on the E Ink display and it works perfectly. And it's not a simple display. It actually changes throughout the day depending on the information hierarchy I need in the morning, in the evening, in the afternoon. And it's able to show the next day that's coming up. It's able to show the weather, which I mean it's Seattle, it's cloudy and rainy. It's able to show relevant quotes from authors that I read frequently, which is important to me because I like to see something that isn't just work. And it has that within a design context that doesn't make me want to throw up because it's all AI slop. And so it's actually really well put together. And the beautiful thing about it is that Fable is able to put that together not just as a design but as a build in a coherent single-threaded build effort where it was like one thread for design, seamless handoff, next thread for build. And you might think, well this is an E Ink display. Nate, you're wasting my time talking about E Ink displays. I don't think that's true. I think that we need to think more about the kinds of jobs that we give these models where we know if we get it right, it would make the day better. It would make the week better. It would lift the load for us. But we have to push the model to the edges to get there. We have to say, I don' fully know what this looks like. You figure it out. Here's a bunch of files.
