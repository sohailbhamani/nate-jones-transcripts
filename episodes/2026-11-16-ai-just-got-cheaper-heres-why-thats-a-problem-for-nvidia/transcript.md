# AI Just Got Cheaper. Here's Why That's a Problem for NVIDIA.

If you've moved work to a cheaper AI model this year, you're part of a problem Nvidia was already worried about back in August. The Wall Street Journal reports Jensen Huang told his finance team that chip wafers and memory Nvidia had committed to were coming at him like a quote avalanche. All of that needs paying customers, and the customers are getting smarter. You found out the cheaper model does most of the job, so you stop paying for the pricey one. I hear that over and over again. Demand for AI is very very real. It's absolutely enormous. What I want to know is whether it is growing at the like 10 x or more pace that pays the bill for the capacity build out. Ramp tracks AI spending by businesses. It reported virtually no growth in token spending across August and September. Two nearly flat months followed by a rise of less than 2% is still extremely slow growth. You're nowhere near the 10-fold increase in spending that you want to see to sustain the kind of build out that the labs are talking about, at least not at that pace. Now, I will call out as someone who's watched the ramp spending numbers that they do bounce around a lot. There are some months when you see 30 40% growth in a month and other months where it's completely flat. What makes this even more interesting is that if you dig into the cross tabs of ramp ramp's most recent report, the most expensive models actually gained spending share in October. So taking a larger share is good for the suppliers of those models, but it doesn't mean the overall market is actually growing quickly enough to sustain the capacity build out. And that's the problem I am interested in. That slow spending growth fits a lot of the conversations I'm having these days. Companies are figuring out that they can move work to a cheaper model over and over again. They test whether it does the job and as models get better, it often does and they use it. They don't need the most intelligent model for every step of the process and they know they don't need to pay for it. And that matches what businesses have told ramp.
