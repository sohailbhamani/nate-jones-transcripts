This is the best news that I have seen for AI in a long, long time. So, we have struggled for ages in computer science to understand what AI means and what it's doing. We've called AI a black box. That's been true for as long as I worked in AI since long before CH GPT. That has changed. What we are realizing is that the way we read AI was predicated on our human assumptions and not on the way machines think. And we're starting to learn how machines think. We kind of have a Rosetta Stone for it. I'm going to give you an example. This is from a real paper from an organization called Good Fire, which is dedicated to safety and alignment, which is great. What they did was they started looking through a vision model and trying to understand the way individual units of computation called neurons work in that vision model. And at first it was confusing. They found a neuron that had a car and a cat. And they were like, "This is why we think that AI is a black box. No one put cars and cats together. Why would you put data together like that? I don't know why the AI makes these decisions." Which is the classic answer for a long time. It happens to work, but we don't know how. Well, when you dig into it, you realize the AI is making sense. It's just operating at a different conceptual level. In the example of the cat and the car, the common concept is a pose. There was a particular pose for cat and car, a particular angle that the computer was was remembering in that instance. That was that was that neuron. It's called a neuron. I know it feels like a biological neuron, but it's not. It could read over 90% of the neurons. Then we're a lot safer. We're a lot safer because we understand how AI makes the decisions it does. So, it's huge green flag for safety.
